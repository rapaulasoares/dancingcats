# Final Investigation Matrix, Scoring, and Educational Logic Audit

## 1. Final Investigation Report

This is the moment the player decides the case is solved and explains what happened. Eight questions, one per screen, locked on submit, no back button, no feedback until the whole report is complete. Option order is shuffled per session.

Design rules:

- **The questions ask the player to explain**, not to recall. In each one, the correct explanation sits among the competing hypotheses the player has been weighing during the game.
- **No question assumes an answer.** The first question does not tell the player that any hypothesis is wrong; a player who still believes in contagion, rats, algae or a supernatural cause finds that explanation among the options and can choose it.
- **Order prevents leaks.** Each question's wording gives away nothing that an earlier, already locked answer did not settle.
- **Wrong options are real hypotheses**, each of which the evidence allows the player to eliminate. None is a joke.
- The forbidden words (the specific metal, the real place and company) stay out; the report says "heavy metal".

### R1 — What happened
**Question:** What best explains the symptoms seen in the residents of Vila do Sol — and why were the "dancing cats" an early warning?

- A. A contagious illness is passing between people and animals that live close together; the cats on the docks caught it first, and neighbours are catching it from them.
- B. The growing number of rats brought a disease into town; the cats that hunt rats caught it first, and rat bites and droppings then spread it to people.
- C. A heavy metal is damaging the nervous system of those who take it in; the cats ate contaminated fish scraps and showed the effects before people did. ✔
- D. Algae multiplied in the warm water in January and released natural poisons; the fish were harmed first, then the cats, birds and people near the bay.
- E. A misfortune fell on the town after the fishermen's statue broke in December; it struck the animals first, as the old stories warn, and then the people.

### R2 — How it reaches people
**Question:** How does the harmful substance reach the people of Vila do Sol?

- A. Through the town's drinking water, which comes from the Serra reservoir in the mountains and is piped to every neighbourhood, so everyone who drinks it is exposed.
- B. Through the skin, when people swim in the bay, wade in the shallows or handle wet nets, and absorb the substance while they work.
- C. Through the rats, which have multiplied and are now all over town: they bite people and leave urine and droppings on food and surfaces.
- D. Through food: it builds up in the fish and shellfish of the bay, and then in the people who eat them often, as the families who eat local seafood daily show. ✔

### R3 — Where it comes from
**Question:** What is the source of the contamination in the bay?

- A. A natural change in the bay itself: the warm week in January heated the water and made the algae multiply, and their toxins stayed in the bay afterwards.
- B. Rat urine: the rats that now run through the streets are washed by the rain into the drains and the river, which carry it into the bay.
- C. Wastewater released by Tucano Industrial through the channel at the north point, where the factory's treated water flows into the bay. ✔
- D. Paint and fuel from the boat-repair yard at the south beach, which have been leaking into the water for years.

### R4 — The rats
**Question:** Why did Vila do Sol suddenly have so many rats?

- A. The rats brought the illness with them, and they multiplied as it spread through the town, feeding on the waste and scraps that sick households left behind.
- B. More food for rats appeared in the streets and around the market, from fish scraps and uncollected rubbish, so the rats had more to eat and bred faster.
- C. A rat infestation at Tucano Industrial spread into the town, as the rats left the factory looking for food and moved into the houses near the harbor.
- D. Many cats became sick or died, so there were far fewer predators hunting the rats, which were free to multiply across the town. ✔

### R5 — João
**Question:** João works at Tucano Industrial every day, yet he and his family are healthy. What does this suggest?

- A. Tucano cannot have anything to do with the problem, because a factory that harmed people would first harm the workers who spend eight hours a day inside it.
- B. Working at the factory does not, by itself, explain who becomes sick, since some factory families are ill and others are not. ✔
- C. João is not telling the truth about his family: he is hiding that someone at home is sick because he is afraid of losing his job.
- D. Factory workers are protected from the illness by their gloves, masks and safety glasses, which is why João's family stays healthy.

### R6 — The company's report
**Question:** Which statement best describes Tucano's internal wastewater report?

- A. It is definitely false, because the company has a reason to hide problems and companies under investigation rarely report the truth.
- B. It proves the wastewater is safe, because it comes from a real laboratory with trained technicians and every measured value is within the permitted limits.
- C. It may be accurate, but it was collected and tested by the company being investigated, so on its own it carries less weight as evidence. ✔
- D. It does not matter, because wastewater is treated before release and treated water has nothing to do with what happens in the bay.

### R7 — The conclusion
**Question:** Which conclusion is best supported by all the evidence?

- A. Algae multiplied in the warm water in January and released natural poisons. The poisons built up in crustaceans and fish, and the cats, gulls and people that ate them developed neurological symptoms.
- B. The rats multiplied and spread a disease through their bites. It affected the cats, the gulls and the people of the town, which is why everything began at the same time as the rise in rats.
- C. Metal from the factory's production process left in its wastewater at the north point and built up in crustaceans and fish. The cats, people and gulls that ate them developed neurological symptoms. ✔
- D. A respected resident explained that the sea is angry since the statue broke. Its anger struck the animals of the bay first, the cats and the gulls, and then the people, so the town should make peace with the sea.

**Reasoning assessed:** joining the whole chain of evidence into one supported conclusion, and telling it apart from three other complete-sounding explanations (algae, rats, a supernatural cause).
**Evidence:** the whole chain — TUC-03, SCI-10, SCI-09, SCI-01, SCI-04, CLI-03, CLI-01, SCI-07.

### R8 — Supporting the conclusion
**Question:** The investigation shows that heavy metal in the factory's wastewater made the animals and people sick. Which group of clues best supports this conclusion?

- A. The factory's own report says every measured value is within the permitted limits, João and his family work there and are healthy, the company says it follows every safety rule, and the water in the channel looks clear.
- B. The rats increased from 8 to 67 between February and June, rats were seen on doorsteps and chewing sacks in the kitchens, fishermen complain that they gnaw holes in the nets, and residents say "the sickness came with the rats".
- C. Most patients live in the streets near the harbor and almost none in the hillside houses near the factory, the Silvas and Marcos's family eat local fish nearly every day, and two families in Porto Azul, who buy fish from the Vila do Sol market, are also sick.
- D. Samples show the most heavy metal in the channel and at its mouth, the animals' blood and tissue tests found methylmercury, the factory uses chemicals with heavy metals, and the affected people eat fish daily. ✔

**Reasoning assessed:** linking a conclusion to the clues that really support it. Every wrong option is a group of real clues from the game: A seems to clear the factory (TUC-02, SQ-05, the clear channel water), B is about the rats (SQ-01, RES-06, RES-08, SQ-03), C shows how the substance reaches people but not where it comes from (CLI-06, RES-01, SQ-07, MKT-04). Only D joins independent measurements (SCI-10, SCI-09), a possible source (TUC-08) and the route (people eat fish).
**Evidence:** SCI-10, SCI-09, TUC-08, CLI-03, RES-01, SQ-06, SQ-07.

## 2. Scoring and Investigator Profile

| Dimension | Inputs |
|---|---|
| Question Quality | Points per question asked (see the Question Matrix); share of strong and useful questions; first-half vs. second-half trend |
| Evidence Analysis | R1, R2; small bonus when at least four internally High items are tagged Important and no more than two red herrings are |
| Logical Reasoning | R5, R7 |
| Source Evaluation | R6, R8; whether the team asked T9 or T6, and P7 |
| Systems Thinking | R3, R4; whether the team collected both SQ-01 and SCI-02, and SCI-01 or SCI-04 |

Each dimension gets one of four levels — **Getting Started, On the Trail, Sharp Investigator, Master Investigator** — and one or two feedback sentences selected by rule. Examples:

| Condition | Feedback |
|---|---|
| At least 60% strong/useful questions | "You asked several specific questions that produced useful evidence." |
| Three or more opinion questions | "You sometimes asked witnesses to guess the cause instead of asking what they observed." |
| Second-half average at least 0.75 points above first-half | "You became more specific with your questions as your investigation progressed." |
| Two or more leading/assumption questions | "Some of your questions suggested their own answer. Neutral questions usually reveal more." |
| R1 answered A | "A contagious illness was a reasonable idea at the start. Look at who cared for the sick, and who stayed well." |
| R1 answered B | "The rats and the illness appeared at about the same time. Look again at which came first." |
| R1 answered D | "The algae did multiply in January. Check when the first strange fish were seen, and what the laboratory found." |
| R6 correct and T9 asked | "You were careful about distinguishing company statements from independent evidence." |
| R6 answered A | "You treated the company's report as false. Being cautious about a source is different from assuming it is lying." |
| R3 and R4 correct | "You identified relationships between different parts of the ecosystem." |
| R5 answered A | "João's health tells you something about factory work. Does it tell you anything about what the factory releases?" |

After the profile, the **case review** walks through the five hypotheses one by one. For each it shows the clues that made it attractive and the evidence that weakens it, including evidence the team never collected (shown as "You did not find this"), then the team's answers beside the best-supported ones.

## 3. Educational Logic Audit

For each conclusion: what lets a student reach it, what was wrong or missing in the original specification, and what was changed. Fixes are evidence, not explanatory text.

### 3.1 What happened (R1)
- **Path to the correct answer:** SCI-09 arrives automatically, so every team holds the heavy-metal finding, the metal in the cat, and the nerve damage. MKT-02 or RES-03 supplies what the cats eat.
- **Gap found:** nothing in the specification linked "heavy metal" to shaking, speech and vision without outside knowledge. **Fix:** SCI-09 reports nerve damage in affected fish and the cat; P5 is the conceptual route.
- **Eliminating contagion:** the specification had only the healthcare-worker clue, behind one question. **Fix:** added Luana (RES-02) and Porto Azul (MKT-04); CLI-01 notes no fever; SCI-09 finds no bacteria.
- **Why the cats came first** is inferable: they live almost entirely on fish scraps (MKT-02) and show the highest level in SCI-09.
- **Remaining risk:** students may ask whether the clinic staff eat local fish. The game does not say. Left open as a discussion point.

### 3.2 How it reaches people (R2)
- **Path:** CLI-03 is the main route. Without it, the same pattern can be assembled from Maria, Mrs. Silva, João and the Costa testimony.
- **Gaps found:** nothing excluded drinking water, air or skin contact. **Fixes:** CLI-05 and the water column in CLI-03; CLI-06, TUC-05 and João for air; for skin contact, non-fishing households that eat local seafood are sick (Oliveira, Costa, Porto Azul buyers) and Luana, in a fishing household, is well.
- **Giveaway removed:** João no longer says "if it were the factory air, wouldn't we all be shaking?"
- **Uncertainty kept:** the Lima row stops the table from being a perfect rule.
- **Build-up** is visible in the SCI-09 numbers even if the team never asked P4. The technical terms are not used until the reveal.

### 3.3 Where it comes from (R3)
- **Largest gap in the specification.** A heavy metal in "a relevant sample" does not point to industry unless the student already knows where heavy metals come from.
- **Fixes:** SCI-10, mud and water samples from five sites (highest inside the channel and at its mouth, low at the river mouth and at the boat-repair yard); P10, where Professor Roberto lists natural and industrial origins; the boat-repair yard on HAR-07 as a second possible human source; SCI-06 and HAR-02 against a natural event.
- **Too obvious?** Tucano is the only company named. Mitigated by timing (SCI-09 and SCI-10 arrive late), by João, and by the ordinary look of the outflow.
- **Now confirmed:** the Science Center sampled the water inside the public channel, outside the factory grounds, so no permission from Tucano was needed. The level falls with distance from the channel. This closes the case: the treated wastewater still carried the heavy metal.

### 3.4 The rats (R4)
- **Path:** SQ-01 (numbers), SCI-02 (concept), and sequence testimony from MKT-05 or RES-04.
- **Problem found:** in the municipal table the two trends are simultaneous, which cannot show direction. **Fix:** two witnesses place the cat decline first; SQ-08 shows as many rats where nobody is sick.
- **Contradiction avoided:** no character says fish scraps now lie uneaten, because that would support option B. Nothing suggests an infestation at Tucano.
- **Historical basis:** in the real case, rats did multiply after the cats died, and fishermen complained that rats gnawed their nets. The game uses this in the newspaper clipping (SQ-03) and in the harbor scene. The monthly counts, the article itself and the rice sack are invented.
- **Known loose end:** a sharp student may ask why rats that eat scraps are not sick. The game is silent; suggested as an extension question in the teacher notes.

### 3.5 João (R5)
- **Path:** SQ-05 and SQ-06 are two separate questions, so a team may hold only half. SQ-07 and CLI-03 cover the gap.
- **Name clash fixed:** the specification's healthy "Santos family" is renamed Almeida.
- **Over-reach guarded:** option A is the tempting wrong answer. The Costa family (factory work, daily seafood, sick) is the evidence against it.

### 3.6 The company's report (R6)
- **Path:** TUC-02 names who collected and who tested; SCI-07 records that independent sampling was not permitted; SCI-03 and TUC-04 show the delay; P8 gives the method.
- **Balance kept:** option A in R6 ("definitely false") is wrong. Mr. Santos's answers are truthful and his reason for the delay is plausible. TUC-05 is a company figure that agrees with independent evidence.

### 3.7 The conclusion and its clues (R7, R8)
- A team with weak evidence can still recognise the form of a careful conclusion, which is the purpose of the question.
- **Length giveaway fixed:** in every question the correct option is no longer than the longest wrong option, and it is not always the shortest either. Wrong options are written as full, plausible explanations.
- **R7 tests the hypothesis; R8 tests the evidence.** R1, R3 and R7 already check that no other hypothesis still convinces the student. R8 states the conclusion and asks which clues support it, so it measures a different skill: linking a claim to the clues that support it. The wrong options are real clues that look relevant but do not support the claim (the company's own report, the rats, the route to people).

### 3.8 Supernatural explanation
- Cannot be disproved by evidence and the game does not try. ROS-01 is a belief; ROS-02 and ROS-03 are accurate observations from the same person, and both are useful. The report asks what "best explains" the evidence, never whether Dona Rosa is wrong.

### 3.9 Evidence that appears to contradict the intended answer
- **Baby Bia (RES-05)** has symptoms and has never eaten fish. Intentional: a real anomaly the evidence does not explain, resolved only in the reveal. No report question depends on it.
- **Lima household:** occasional seafood, unclear symptoms. Intentional noise.
- **M6:** Maria agrees the algae "could be" the cause, contradicting her own timeline in M1. Intentional demonstration of a leading question.

### 3.10 Solvability with poor questioning
- The 27 hotspot and automatic items alone provide the outflow, the animal data, the food chain, the water notice, the lab results and the sampling map. A team that asks only weak questions can answer R1, R3 and R4 with effort, but will lack the diet pattern needed for R2 and will see that reflected in the profile.
- Every High item needed for a report answer has at least two routes, except CLI-02 (covered by three alternative items) and SCI-07 (partly covered by T9, TUC-04 and SCI-03).

### 3.11 Competing hypotheses and elimination
- **Problem found:** the first version gave the wrong hypotheses only one or two attractive clues each, so environmental contamination stood out too early.
- **Fix:** each wrong hypothesis now has at least four clues that pull toward it and at least four that allow it to be ruled out (see the Elimination map in the Evidence Matrix).
- **Order matters:** the attractive clues are easy hotspots; most eliminating clues need a good question or arrive late.
- **Every misleading clue is true.** The cases did rise, the school did close, rats did gnaw the sack, the statue did break, algae did multiply once. A true fact can point the wrong way, usually because it only shows that two things happened together.
- **Never named during play:** the five hypotheses are not shown to the player anywhere before the Final Report. The player meets them only as clues, rumours and their own ideas. The first report question is where they appear side by side.

### 3.12 Questions that assumed knowledge the player did not have
- **Problem found:** several interview questions referred to facts the player might not have met (the wastewater sample, the Friday visit, rats, the algae). The first final question had the same flaw: it assumed contagion was already ruled out.
- **Fix:** Mr. Santos's interview was rewritten; follow-up and knowledge-gated questions were introduced; P7 was reworded; the report now opens with R1.
- **Rule for all future content:** a question may refer only to what the opening story, the current scene, or the player's own evidence and earlier answers have already shown.

### 3.13 Accidental giveaways checked
- No evidence title states a conclusion (for example "Municipal animal observations", not "Cats down, rats up").
- The outflow photograph shows clear water and an ordinary channel.
- Maria does not mention the channel; the player must connect "north point" with the harbor chart.
- The lab report names a contaminant family only. Forbidden words are enforced by an automated test.
- R2 says "the harmful substance" and R3 says "the contamination". Both follow R1, which is already locked.
